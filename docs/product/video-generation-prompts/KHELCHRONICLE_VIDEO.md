Distinguish peer-reviewed research, preprints, official records, expert commentary and journalism. Do not describe a preprint as peer-reviewed. Do not overstate causation from correlation or generalise beyond the evidence.

## Editorial structure

1. State the research question and why it matters.
2. Define key terms and scope.
3. Explain the evidence and methods in accessible language.
4. Compare findings and note disagreement or uncertainty.
5. Explain practical implications without overstating them.
6. Identify limitations and unanswered questions.
7. Provide a balanced conclusion and direct viewers to sources.

## Visual plan

Use original diagrams, clearly attributed data visualisations, generic illustrative sports footage and approved interview clips. Do not invent datasets, charts, quotations, paper findings or study participants. Add data labels and citations in post-production and ensure they match the source.

## Editorial safeguards

- Obtain appropriate permissions for interviews and third-party figures.
- Check whether images, charts or tables may be reused.
- Disclose funding, sponsorship and relevant conflicts.
- Avoid medical, performance or selection advice beyond the evidence and qualifications available.
- Correct errors transparently.

## Final review

A subject-matter reviewer checks evidence and interpretation; an editor checks clarity and balance; rights review checks media reuse; brand review checks KhelChronicle / KhelGrid identity.
