Create original, useful, accurate and professionally produced video content for KhelGrid, an Indian sports discovery and athlete-development platform.

- Primary navy: `#0B192C`
- Primary emerald: `#10B981`
- Supporting colours: white, charcoal and natural sports-environment colours.
- Personality: ambitious, authentic, inclusive, informative and inspiring.
- Visual language: cinematic sports documentary, realistic action, purposeful camera movement and clean editorial graphics.
- Communication: clear, credible and accessible to Indian audiences.

Do not imply that KhelGrid provides a service, partnership, verification or opportunity unless that capability or relationship has been confirmed.

## 3. Master generation prompt

Create an original, high-quality cinematic sports video for KhelGrid, an Indian sports discovery and athlete-development platform. Communicate **[CORE MESSAGE]** through **[SPORT / SUBJECT]** for **[TARGET AUDIENCE]**.

Use fictional, non-identifiable athletes unless documented permissions authorize otherwise. Show realistic sporting environments, accurate equipment, correct technique, natural body mechanics and believable physical movement. Use purposeful camera movement, consistent lighting, natural textures and realistic depth of field. Apply subtle navy (`#0B192C`) and emerald (`#10B981`) accents where appropriate.

Follow the supplied script and shot list. Preserve continuity in athlete appearance, clothing, equipment, environment, lighting, movement direction and camera geography between shots. Leave clean composition space for titles and captions to be added in post-production.

Do not generate embedded text, logos, watermarks, third-party trademarks, fake sponsor marks, fabricated statistics or identifiable real people without appropriate permission. Do not recreate copyrighted broadcasts, viral clips, named creators' distinctive styles or protected tournament branding.

## 4. Script structure

Adapt the structure to the format rather than forcing every video into the same duration.

1. **Hook:** a compelling question, visual or statement relevant to the audience.
2. **Context:** explain the subject and why it matters.
3. **Main story:** organise the information into clearly sequenced sections.
4. **Evidence / demonstration:** use verified sources, accurate demonstrations or clearly labelled illustrative scenes.
5. **Takeaway:** explain what the viewer should remember or do.
6. **Close:** concise conclusion and relevant call to action.

Every factual claim, statistic, date, record, quote and named organisation must be checked against reliable sources. Keep a source list with the script.

## 5. Shot-by-shot plan

For each shot, record:

- Shot number and target duration
- Narrative purpose
- Visual subject and action
- Environment and time of day
- Athlete / character continuity description
- Camera framing, angle and movement
- Lens / depth-of-field intent (if supported)
- Lighting and weather
- Voice-over / dialogue
- Ambient sound and effects
- Transition into and out of the shot
- Reference frame or source asset
- Generation prompt and model settings
- Review notes

Generate complex sequences as separate clips when a model cannot maintain reliable continuity. Review each clip before assembling the edit.

## 6. Sports realism and continuity

Check sport-specific rules, field/court dimensions, equipment, grip, stance, technique, player positions and ball/shuttle trajectories. Use a sport specialist or reliable reference when uncertain.

Maintain consistent clothing, athlete appearance, equipment, environment, weather, lighting, screen direction and time of day. Avoid impossible physics, unsafe demonstrations presented as instruction, or misleading visual depictions of real competitions.

## 7. Camera and lighting

Use wide shots to establish place, medium shots for action, close-ups for detail and technique, tracking shots to follow movement, and static framing for interviews or explainers. Use slow motion only when it adds meaning and the source supports it.

Lighting must remain physically consistent within a scene. Avoid excessive camera shake, artificial glow, unnecessary transitions and visual effects that obscure the action.

## 8. Audio and narration

Plan voice-over, dialogue, ambience, sport-specific effects and music as separate layers. Keep narration intelligible, avoid clipping, balance music below speech, and verify pronunciation of Indian names and sport terminology.

Record the source and permissions for music, sound effects, voice talent and synthetic voices. Do not imitate a real person's voice without appropriate authorization. Do not use unlicensed broadcast commentary.

## 9. Brand graphics and safe zones

Add the official KhelGrid logo, titles, lower-thirds, subtitles, verified statistics and calls to action in post-production using approved assets. Do not ask a video model to render final logos or text.

Use navy and emerald consistently, maintain readable contrast and safe margins, and keep graphics clear of important action. Do not invent sponsors, official competition graphics, platform capabilities or endorsements.

## 10. Rights, safety and editorial integrity

- Use fictional, non-identifiable people unless releases and permissions are documented.
- Check rights for footage, images, reference frames, music, fonts, templates and other inputs.
- Review the AI provider's applicable terms, commercial-use conditions and restrictions.
- Do not imitate protected footage or present illustrative AI scenes as genuine documentary evidence.
- Do not fabricate interviews, athlete achievements, event participation, competition results or venue access.
- Verify facts and attribute sources where appropriate.
- Obtain permissions for identifiable people, private locations and other protected material where required.
- Apply appropriate disclosures for realistic synthetic or altered content where platform rules or law require them.

AI generation alone does not establish exclusive copyright ownership, non-infringement or production approval. Retain prompts, original outputs, tool/model details, terms evidence, edits and review records.

## 11. Delivery specifications

**Horizontal long-form:** 16:9, normally 1920×1080 minimum; 4K where justified.  
**Vertical short-form:** 9:16, normally 1080×1920.  
**Frame rate:** match source footage and intended motion; commonly 24/25/30/50/60 fps. Do not convert frame rates unnecessarily.  
**Delivery:** MP4 with a widely compatible video codec such as H.264 and AAC audio; retain high-quality masters and editable project files.

Create separate horizontal and vertical edits where composition requires it. Do not rely on automatic cropping alone. Check current platform upload and Shorts eligibility requirements before publishing.

## 12. Accessibility and language

Review captions and transcripts; do not rely on uncorrected automatic transcription. Use readable text, sufficient contrast and safe caption placement. Explain specialist terms, verify translations and prepare Hindi or other language versions where useful.

## 13. Negative prompt

Avoid distorted anatomy, extra limbs, unstable faces, warped equipment, incorrect rules, impossible ball physics, floating objects, morphing, flicker, abrupt lighting changes, inconsistent uniforms, fake text, gibberish signage, logos, watermarks, fake sponsors, unlicensed trademarks, fabricated results, misleading documentary scenes, excessive motion blur, oversaturated colours and plastic-looking skin.

## 14. Production workflow and gates

1. **Plan:** brief, audience, objective, research and source verification.
2. **Script:** narration, structure, storyboard and shot list.
3. **Generate / film:** record model, version, prompts, source inputs and outputs.
4. **Edit:** assemble, grade, mix audio, add approved graphics and captions.
5. **Review:** sports accuracy, factual accuracy, continuity, accessibility and technical quality.
6. **Rights review:** visual, audio, likeness, location and AI-provider terms.
7. **Approval:** editorial, rights and brand sign-off.
8. **Publish:** platform-specific export, accurate title/description/thumbnail and required disclosures.
9. **Archive:** retain masters, project files, source references and metadata.

## 15. Approval rule

Do not mark an asset production-approved until factual, sports, visual, audio, rights, accessibility and brand reviews are complete and recorded. A draft prompt or generated clip is not publication approval.
