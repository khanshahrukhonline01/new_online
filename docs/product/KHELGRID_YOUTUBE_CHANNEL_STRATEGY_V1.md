# KhelGrid YouTube Channel Strategy v1.0

**Status:** Draft for review

## 1. Executive direction

Build the KhelGrid YouTube channel as a useful, credible Indian sports discovery and athlete-development media channel. The channel should help athletes, parents, coaches and sports communities understand sports pathways, opportunities, venues, training concepts and the wider sporting ecosystem.

The channel is not a generic highlights feed and must not imply official relationships with leagues, federations, academies or athletes unless those relationships are documented.

**Positioning statement**

> KhelGrid helps India's sporting community discover opportunities, understand the game and make more informed decisions about athlete development.

**Editorial promise**

Every upload should deliver at least one of the following:
- A practical explanation.
- A verified piece of sports information.
- A useful pathway or discovery guide.
- A thoughtful story or research-based perspective.
- A transparent demonstration of a real KhelGrid feature.

Do not publish merely to meet a schedule. Accuracy, usefulness and rights clearance take priority over volume.

## 2. Audience and channel positioning

### Primary audiences

1. **Athletes and aspiring athletes:** looking for pathways, trials, development information and practical sports knowledge.
2. **Parents and guardians:** seeking clear, responsible information about youth sports participation and opportunities.
3. **Coaches and academies:** interested in training education, athlete development and the sports ecosystem.
4. **Organisers and venue operators:** interested in discovery, participation and sports infrastructure.
5. **Sports-interested viewers:** looking for accessible explainers, stories and research-led content about Indian sport.

### Channel personality

- Helpful, informed, energetic and respectful.
- Ambitious without exaggerated promises.
- Inclusive of different sports, regions, genders, ages and participation levels.
- Clear enough for newcomers while remaining useful to experienced viewers.
- English-first at launch, with Hindi versions or subtitles introduced after translation review and production capacity are established.

### What the channel is not

- Not an official news broadcaster or live-score service.
- Not a substitute for a federation's official rules, selection notices or event announcements.
- Not a guarantee of selection, scholarships, employment, bookings or sporting success.
- Not a place for unverified trial notices, fabricated testimonials, invented statistics or recycled broadcast footage.

## 3. Channel identity and setup

### Recommended channel name

**KhelGrid**

Use the same spelling and brand identity across the channel, website and social accounts. Check handle availability before promising a specific handle.

### Suggested channel tagline

**Discover opportunities. Understand the game. Develop your journey.**

### Channel description draft

KhelGrid is an Indian sports discovery and athlete-development platform. This channel shares useful sports explainers, athlete-development guidance, opportunity and pathway information, venue and coaching discovery guidance, sports stories and research-led perspectives.

Our goal is to make sports information easier to understand and help athletes, parents, coaches and the wider sporting community make more informed decisions.

We aim to verify factual information, identify sources and distinguish illustrative visuals from genuine footage. Always confirm dates, eligibility, fees and application details with the relevant official organiser before acting on an opportunity.

Explore KhelGrid: https://khelgrid.com/

### Channel visual assets

- **Profile image:** approved KhelGrid logo, legible at small circular sizes.
- **Banner:** original sports-themed composition using navy and emerald, with a concise channel promise. Keep important elements inside YouTube's current cross-device safe area.
- **Video watermark:** approved simplified logo; avoid making it intrusive.
- **Thumbnails:** use the standards in Section 7.
- **Opening / closing:** short, restrained brand animation added in editing; do not delay the video hook with a long intro.

Check YouTube's current image dimensions, safe areas and upload requirements when producing assets; platform specifications can change.

## 4. Content pillars

Use six recurring pillars. The percentages are planning targets for the first 90 days, not rigid quotas.

| Pillar | Planning share | Editorial focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Sports education and explainers | 25% | Rules, formats, terminology, techniques and how sport works |
| Opportunities and pathways | 20% | How to find, evaluate and verify trials, scholarships, camps and development routes |
| Athlete development | 20% | Training principles, preparation, recovery literacy and long-term development |
| Indian sports stories and ecosystem | 15% | Sports history, disciplines, regional ecosystems and evidence-led features |
| Venues, coaching and participation | 10% | How to assess facilities, coaching options and safe participation |
| KhelGrid and KhelChronicle | 10% | Transparent product walkthroughs and research-led editorial work |

A video may serve more than one pillar, but assign one primary pillar for planning and analytics.

### Pillar rules

**Sports education:** Use authoritative rules and subject-matter review for technical explanations. State when rules differ by age group, competition or governing body.

**Opportunities and pathways:** Verify the organiser, source URL, publication date, deadline, location, eligibility, fees and application process. If a detail cannot be confirmed, say so. Never create a fictional opportunity to fill a calendar.

**Athlete development:** Distinguish general education from individual coaching, medical or nutrition advice. Use qualified review for higher-risk training instruction.

**Stories and ecosystem:** Verify names, dates, achievements and quotes. Obtain contributor releases and footage permissions. Label illustrative AI visuals.

**Venues and coaching:** Do not imply endorsement or verification without a documented review process. Confirm details with the provider before presenting them as current.

**KhelGrid / KhelChronicle:** Demonstrate only features that are live and verified. Clearly label planned or unavailable capabilities. KhelChronicle research videos must distinguish peer-reviewed studies, preprints, official data, expert commentary and journalism.

## 5. Formats and publishing model

### Core formats

- **Long-form:** explainers, guides, interviews, documentaries and research features.
- **Shorts:** one question, one useful point, one concise takeaway.
- **Playlists:** group videos by audience need and content pillar.
- **Community posts:** use for polls, topic requests and published-video discussion only when the channel has access to the feature and moderation capacity.

### Recommended launch cadence

A sustainable initial target, subject to available production capacity:

- 1 long-form video per week.
- 2 Shorts per week, preferably cut from or supporting the long-form subject.
- 1 monthly editorial review.

This is a target, not a promise. If quality, verification or rights review cannot be completed, delay the upload rather than publish incomplete work. Batch production where possible.

## 7. Titles and thumbnail standards

### Title principles

- Lead with the viewer's question, need or clear benefit.
- Use plain language and accurate specificity.
- Include the sport or subject where it helps discovery.
- Avoid keyword stuffing, false urgency and exaggerated promises.
- Do not imply guaranteed selection, scholarships, income, performance gains or access.
- Keep the title aligned with what the video actually delivers.
- Use a consistent language choice for each upload; avoid awkward keyword lists.

**Useful title patterns**
- How to [complete a real task] in [context]
- [Topic] Explained: What Athletes and Parents Should Know
- Before You Apply: [Specific verification checklist]
- What [Sport / Rule / Format] Actually Means
- [Question]? A Clear Guide for [Audience]

### Thumbnail principles

- One main subject or visual idea.
- Strong contrast using the KhelGrid navy and emerald palette.
- Short, readable wording; usually 2–5 words, not a second title paragraph.
- Use original or rights-cleared images and approved brand assets.
- Keep faces and key action clear; do not use a real person's likeness without permission.
- Ensure the design remains legible at small mobile sizes.
- Do not fabricate event scenes, trophies, results, selection letters or platform screens.
- Avoid fake arrows, misleading circles, false before/after imagery and sensational expressions.

### Thumbnail layout system

1. **Explainer:** one sport-related visual + short question or concept.
2. **Checklist / guide:** one clear subject + concise action phrase.
3. **Documentary:** authentic or licensed scene + editorial title.
4. **KhelGrid walkthrough:** genuine current product screenshot + accurate feature label.
5. **KhelChronicle:** research visualisation or subject image + precise topic label.

### Thumbnail quality checklist

- [ ] Matches the video and title.
- [ ] Main subject is immediately understandable.
- [ ] Text is readable on a phone.
- [ ] No unsupported claim or fake result.
- [ ] Visual rights and likeness permissions are recorded.
- [ ] Logo use follows the approved brand asset.
- [ ] Crop and contrast are checked at small size.

Use YouTube's current thumbnail specifications and policies at export time.

## 8. Description, captions and publishing checklist

### Description structure

1. Two-sentence summary of the actual video.
2. Key points or chapters where useful.
3. Sources and official links supporting factual claims.
4. Relevant KhelGrid link: https://khelgrid.com/
5. Clear note where visuals are illustrative or AI-generated, when needed.
6. Corrections / contact route and relevant disclosures.
7. A small number of genuinely relevant hashtags.

Do not add unrelated tags, misleading links or unsupported claims. Verify every URL before publishing.

### Before publishing

- [ ] Script and factual claims reviewed.
- [ ] Subject-matter review completed where needed.
- [ ] Music, footage, images, fonts and voice permissions documented.
- [ ] AI-generated / altered-content disclosure requirements checked.
- [ ] Thumbnail and title accurately represent the content.
- [ ] Captions and transcript corrected.
- [ ] Description links and source references checked.
- [ ] No private or sensitive personal information exposed.
- [ ] Final export watched in full on desktop and mobile.
- [ ] Editorial, rights and brand approvals recorded.

### Weekly operating rhythm

- **Week before publication:** research, verify and approve the script.

- **After publication:** review early audience signals and note questions for future content.

Avoid publishing a video on a fixed date if its source material, permissions or accuracy checks are incomplete.

## 10. Production workflow and responsibilities

### Workflow

1. **Topic selection:** choose from the pillars based on audience need, relevance, evidence and production capacity.
2. **Research:** collect authoritative sources; record dates and limitations.
3. **Editorial brief:** define audience, viewer promise, format and CTA.
4. **Script and storyboard:** write narration, scenes, shot list and on-screen text.
5. **Review:** subject-matter and editorial review before generation or filming.
6. **Production:** use the relevant category template and master video framework.
7. **Post-production:** edit, colour grade, mix audio, add approved graphics and captions.
8. **Rights and quality assurance:** check visuals, audio, likeness, location, factual accuracy, accessibility and technical export.
9. **Approval:** record editorial, rights and brand sign-off.
10. **Publishing:** upload with accurate title, thumbnail, description, captions and disclosures.
11. **Measurement:** review performance and audience feedback.
12. **Archive and correction:** retain project files and sources; correct material errors transparently.

### Suggested responsibility matrix

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Channel / editorial lead | Strategy, topic approval, final editorial sign-off |
| Researcher / scriptwriter | Sources, script, fact-check record |
| Sports subject reviewer | Rules, technique and specialist accuracy |
| Producer / editor | Filming or generation, edit, audio and exports |
| Rights reviewer | Footage, music, likeness, location and AI terms |
| Brand reviewer | Logo, palette, thumbnail and CTA consistency |
| Channel publisher | Metadata, upload, captions, links and post-publish checks |

One person may hold multiple roles in a small team, but the review steps must still be completed and recorded. Do not self-approve a high-risk factual or rights question without an appropriate second review.

## 11. Performance measurement

Review monthly rather than judging the channel from one upload.

### Core metrics

- Impressions and click-through rate (CTR), interpreted by traffic source and audience.
- Views and unique viewers.
- Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- Audience-retention curve and key drop-off points.
- Returning viewers and subscriber changes.
- Shorts viewed-versus-swiped-away and average view duration, where available.
- Comments, shares and meaningful audience questions.
- Clicks to KhelGrid links where measurement is configured transparently and lawfully.

### Learning questions

- Which topics attract the intended audience?
- Where do viewers stop watching?
- Do titles and thumbnails accurately set expectations?
- Which questions recur in comments?
- Are viewers finding the next relevant video or playlist?
- Does the production effort justify the audience value?

Do not buy views, subscribers or engagement. Do not use misleading thumbnails or engagement bait. Do not treat views alone as proof of business impact.

## 12. Community, moderation and safeguarding

- Publish clear comment moderation rules.
- Remove spam, scams, harassment and unsafe personal-data disclosures.
- Never request a minor's private contact details in public comments.
- Do not publish personal information about athletes without a clear lawful basis and appropriate permission.
- Direct opportunity applicants to official organiser channels, not unverified intermediaries.
- Do not promise selection, representation, scholarships or personal replies.
- Escalate safeguarding concerns through an established internal process.
